1Executive Summary

The month in plain language: what happened, what it means, what’s next.

Between October 12 and November 10, LinkedIn campaigns spent $3,019 and delivered 105,777 impressions, 116 clicks and 1,606 video views. Image and video ads drew a 0.41% click-through rate, level with September. This was the first full month on the audiences rebuilt at the end of September around Director- and VP-level marketing leaders. Reaching them got cheaper: the cost per click on the main image ads was $24.19, below September’s $27.11. No conversion events were recorded this month.

Wins: The Director + Event titles audience delivered clicks at $19.12, the lowest top-of-funnel cost in the account. The “upgrade your events experience” ad was the best performer at 0.64% click-through. Financial-services companies engaged at the highest rates of any industry. The video held a 41.9% view rate, well above the ~25% benchmark. The first bottom-of-funnel ads went live in the second half of the month. VP and Director-level members made up 85% of cold-audience impressions and 92% of clicks, and their account-wide share rose from 66% to 78% after the audience rebuild.
Watch-outs: No conversion events this month (4 in September). The retargeting audiences reached fewer people, with members reached down 55–70%. That limits how much the middle and bottom of the funnel can grow. Video viewers watched about 9 seconds per view, down from 10.

2Optimizations Applied This Month

The concrete work done this month, stated exactly as performed.
  • Rebalanced bids and budgets between the two top-of-funnel image audiences so both could deliver steadily, and adjusted them through the month to keep spend on pace.
  • Paused the weakest image ads, mostly abstract message angles, to concentrate delivery on the messages that earned more clicks.
  • Refreshed creative across the top-of-funnel image, Text Ad and Spotlight ad sets with new message angles, to counter creative fatigue.
  • Launched personalized versions of the video ads and retired the lower-performing video cuts.
  • Launched the bottom-of-funnel layer: single-image ads to the warm audience, each linked to the matching service page. Budgets on other ad sets were trimmed to fund it.
  • Added competitor agencies to DemandSense competitor monitoring, to track rival activity alongside our own.
  • Reviewed conversion tracking: confirmed the Insight Tag fires on all target pages and website conversions are active, and identified that the server-side connection (Conversions API) is not yet receiving CRM events.
  • Monitored retargeting audience size after a drop in the matched audience, linked to creative fatigue, and refreshed creative to address it.
Context for the numbers: The bottom-of-funnel layer launched in the second half of the month and serves a higher-cost audience. Budgets on the Director + Event titles audience and on video were trimmed to make room, which explains their lower spend against September.

9Red Flags & Risks

What needs attention, ranked by urgency.
🔴 High priority

No conversion events this month

The account recorded no conversion events this month (4 in September). We can see engagement and which companies interact with the ads, but not yet which of them become leads or opportunities. The CRM review in §10 is the next step toward connecting that engagement to pipeline.
🔴 High priority

Retargeting audiences reached fewer people

Members reached fell from 847 to 341 on video, 1,098 to 326 on Spotlight and 756 to 339 on Text Ads. A small warm pool limits how much budget the middle and bottom of the funnel can use well.
🟡 Medium priority

High frequency on the retargeting ad sets (11.2 to 25.2)

The same small pool sees the ads many times. Video click-through dipped from 0.52% to 0.42% while frequency rose, an early sign of creative fatigue.
🟡 Medium priority

Video viewers leave early

Viewers watched about 9 seconds per view of a 64-second video, down from 10 seconds in September, so most see only the opening.
🟡 Medium priority

Some volume goes to segments that do not engage

C-suite, Business Development and Operations titles, and healthcare-related industries took a meaningful share of impressions with little or no engagement. In the warm audience, about 20% of impressions reached companies under 200 employees. We will address this with exclusions (see §10).

10Recommendations & Next Steps

The plan for Nov 11 – Dec 11, by priority, one action per item.
🔴 High

Use CRM data to decide which audiences to keep and which to pause

The HubSpot connection is live and shows the companies in CG’s CRM, but not yet the deals or their stages. A CRM export for the past 90 days would let us compare new contacts and deals with the companies, industries, company sizes and job titles our campaigns have been reaching. A lead can’t always be traced to a single campaign. Even so, seeing where CRM activity overlaps with the audiences we target would show which targeting is most closely aligned with real opportunities. With that, we can weigh which audiences to keep and grow and which to pause, so budget decisions reflect business outcomes as well as clicks and engagement. Completing the Web ID tag on the site will add more detail on which companies visit after seeing the ads.
🔴 High

Apply exclusions to segments that are not engaging

This addresses the red flag in §9. We will exclude the titles, functions and industries that drew volume this month with little or no engagement. We will also add company-size and seniority filters to the retargeting audiences, so spend stays focused on decision-makers at the right companies.
🔴 High

Introduce new video angles, including shorter 15–30 second versions

The video continues to earn a strong 41.9% view rate, so the opening is clearly capturing attention. Most viewers, however, leave before the end: only 2.6% of views reach completion, and watch time eased from 10.2 to 9.1 seconds per view as frequency rose. We’d like to test shorter 15–30 second versions against the current video, alongside a fresh angle or two. That would show whether a tighter edit carries the full message to more of the audience.
🟡 Medium

Run one creative test per funnel stage each month (test)

To build a clear winning concept at every stage, we’ll run one controlled test per stage each month:
  • Top of funnel: the personalized versus standard “upgrade your events” ad in the VP & Manager audience.
  • Middle of funnel: the shorter 15–30 second video versions against the current cut.
  • Bottom of funnel: “Learn more” against the current “Request Demo” call to action, to see whether a call to action that matches the service page lifts response among warm visitors.
Each month’s report will name the winning concept for each stage.
🟡 Medium

Test weighting the top of the funnel toward 1,001–5,000-employee companies (test)

In both cold audiences, companies with 1,001–5,000 employees clicked through more often than those with 501–1,000. We will test a heavier weighting toward this band and keep it if the results hold.
🟡 Medium

Align targeting with CG’s full customer list (test)

The current industry targeting is performing well, with financial services leading engagement this month. LinkedIn groups companies into industries using its own classification, which doesn’t always match how CG would describe its clients. A company CG knows as a technology or professional-services client, for example, may sit under a different industry on LinkedIn. Mapping CG’s full customer list, current and past, against LinkedIn’s classification would show us strong-fit industries that the current campaign data doesn’t make obvious. We can then test those alongside the existing targeting and widen it where they perform. The same list also gives us a reliable way to keep all existing clients out of prospecting.
